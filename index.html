Quant Developer in 104 Weeks

A schedule built for one hour on weekday evenings and two to three at the weekend — designed for the tired hour after a nine-to-five, not the imaginary one. Week 1 starts whenever you start. Tick weeks off as you finish them; progress saves on this device.

What was decided, and what it cost

01 · Scope

Every line below was a fork with a cheaper alternative. They are written down so that in month nine, when this feels wrong, you can argue with the reasoning instead of with yourself.

DecisionChoice, and the road not taken
RoleQuant developer. Not researcher, not trader. Researcher means competing on statistics against PhDs, which is your weakest axis; developer means competing on engineering, which is the asset you already own.
SpecialisationMarket microstructure and execution. Systematic infrastructure secondary. Derivatives pricing is literacy only — enough to talk, never enough to price exotics.
LanguageFull C++ pivot. The expensive branch: roughly 150–200 hours, and it means competing partly as a junior. The JVM-first alternative would reach interviews a year sooner but not reach prop shops. Week 34 is the off-ramp.
MarketEquities and traditional finance. Jakarta first, Singapore and remote in scope, relocation acceptable.
CryptoOut as a career target, in as a data source. It is the only free full-depth real-time order book data on earth. Nobody in an interview cares which venue's bytes your engine parsed.
CredentialsNone. No CQF, no bootcamp. A master's is deferred until after landing in the industry, ideally funded by someone else.
Stepping stoneAccepted, at any pay, from week 36. Being inside the industry beats being outside it with better notes.

Why the number is five, not ten

02 · Reality

Here is the arithmetic that shapes everything else, so you can check it rather than trust it.

Weekdays — 5 × 1 hr5 hrs / wk +Weekend — 2 × 2.5 hr5 hrs / wk Available10 hrs / wk

But the hours are not fungible. Studying after a full working day means the weekday hour carries low to medium cognitive load — drilling, reading, review. New mathematics does not happen at 9pm. It never has, for anyone.

So your real capacity for new concepts is about 5 hours a week, not 10. Over 104 weeks that is roughly 520 hours of genuine learning plus 520 hours of practice and repetition. It is enough for one specialisation done properly. It is not enough for two, which is why the scope above is so narrow.

The one lucky break: C++ drilling genuinely works at 8pm. It is muscle memory rather than insight. That is a real argument for the C++ pivot that has nothing to do with job listings.

Two ways this length changes

  • The diagnostic comes back strong. If probability is still largely intact, the maths block drops from 8 weeks to 4 and everything downstream pulls forward by a month. Sit the test before believing any date on this page.
  • Your floor is below 10 hours. If a normal bad week is 6 hours, this is a 170-week plan and pretending otherwise just moves the disappointment later. Log real hours for four weeks before trusting the length.

The weekly engine

03 · Rhythm

The phases change what you study. This does not change. It has to survive tired evenings, so it is built to be boring and automatic.

Weekday — 60 minutes, same slot every evening

MinutesBlockWhat you actually do
0–10ReviewNever skipSpaced repetition on C++ semantics and probability facts. Ten minutes, cards only. This is the compounding part.
10–50C++ / project codeUntil week 30, a learncpp section plus its exercises. From week 31, the flagship project. Code every weekday, however little.
50–60ReadingHarris, a paper, an exchange spec, a talk. The only block you may move to a commute.

One weekday a week from week 14, the reading block becomes a 15-minute recorded explanation in English of a technical decision you made. Unscripted, then listened back. Roughly ten weeks of discomfort, then it stops being hard.

Saturday — 150 minutes, the only hard block

MinutesBlockWhat you actually do
0–15Cold recallRedo last week's hardest problem without notes. If you cannot, that topic repeats this week.
15–90New mathematicsProblems, not videos. Watching a lecture and understanding it is not the same as being able to do it, and only one of those is tested.
90–150Hard project workDesign, the difficult implementation, benchmarking. The part that needs a whole brain.

Sunday — 150 minutes, ending in a commit

MinutesBlockWhat you actually do
0–90Project workImplementation, tests, cleanup. Ship something small every Sunday.
90–130Microstructure readingHarris, the LOB survey, exchange rulebooks, execution papers.
130–150Log and commitNever skipHours, what happened, what is stuck. Commit it publicly even when the honest entry is “did nothing.” A missing entry is the only real failure mode.

The toolkit

05 · Materials

One primary source per topic at a time. Collecting resources is procrastination that feels like progress.

Blitzstein & Hwang — Introduction to Probability Free · Phase 1 The single most important book in the maths track, and the free PDF plus Harvard's Stat 110 lectures are the whole course. Conditional expectation and the tower property are taught better here than anywhere else. Do the problem sets; watching the lectures is not the same thing.
learncpp.com Free · Phase 1 Free, structured, current, and the best zero-to-competent C++ path that exists. Front to back, no skipping. Your Java instincts about object lifetime and memory will actively mislead you here, which is exactly why it takes 200 hours.
Stroustrup — A Tour of C++ (3rd ed.) Paid · Phase 1 Short and dense. Read it immediately after learncpp, when you have enough context for compression to be a feature rather than a wall.
Meyers — Effective Modern C++ Paid · Phase 2 One item per weekday evening. Move semantics, perfect forwarding and smart pointers are interview territory and you will be asked about all three.
Williams — C++ Concurrency in Action (2nd ed.) Paid · Phase 2 The memory model chapter is the important one. You have written threaded Java, which means the concepts transfer and the details will trip you repeatedly — the JVM gave you guarantees C++ does not.
Harris — Trading and Exchanges Paid · Phase 2 The most important book in the entire plan. Non-mathematical, readable, and it is precisely what practitioners mean when they say someone “understands markets.” If you buy one thing on this page, buy this.
Gould et al. — Limit Order Books Free · Phase 2 The survey that maps the whole field. Read it once at the start of Phase 2 and again at the end; the second reading is a different paper because you are a different reader.
NASDAQ ITCH & FIX specifications Free · Phase 2 Public documents that most candidates have never opened. Read one end to end. Knowing what an add-order message actually contains is worth more in an interview than any course certificate.
IDX rulebook and trading regulations Free · Phase 2 Sessions, tick-size bands, auto-rejection limits, settlement, fees. No imported curriculum covers Jakarta's microstructure and every local interview will. This is the cheapest edge available to you.
LOBSTER sample data Free · Phase 3 Free reconstructed NASDAQ order book days. This is your ground truth: if your engine replays a LOBSTER day and matches, it is correct in a way you can prove rather than assert.
Crypto exchange WebSocket L2 feeds Free · Phase 4 Binance, OKX or Coinbase. The only free, full-depth, real-time order book data an individual can get. Career target says equities; the bytes can come from anywhere.
Almgren & Chriss — Optimal Execution Free · Phase 5 The execution paper. Read it properly, implement the schedule, then explain in your own words why it beats a naive TWAP. That explanation is an interview answer.
Crack — Heard on the Street · Zhou — green book Paid · Phase 6 Interview brainteasers and probability puzzles. Worthless to start early and essential in the last four months. Joshi's Quant Job Interview Questions is the more C++-flavoured third option and suits this target best.
Total spend Paid · ~6 books Harris and learncpp cover the first six months between them, and only Harris costs money. Buy Meyers and Williams around week 15, Hull around week 80, the interview books around week 88. Everything else on this page is free.

Rules that make it survive

07 · Discipline

Two years is long enough that discipline will fail somewhere. These decide what happens when it does — written now, while you are still enthusiastic and therefore still honest.

The minimum viable day is ten minutes

On a bad day, clear the review queue and stop. This is not failure, it is the designed floor. A ten-minute day keeps the habit alive; a zero day costs three days of restarting.

The Sunday commit is the only non-negotiable

Everything else in this plan can slip. The log entry cannot. Commit it publicly even when it reads “did nothing this week,” because the honest entry is data and the missing entry is a plan quietly dying.

Three missed entries means the plan is wrong

Not that you are lazy. Rewrite the plan to fit the life you actually have rather than trying harder against a schedule that has already been disproved.

Bad week protocol: keep C++, drop the mathematics

When a week collapses, keep the weekday drilling and abandon the weekend block. Skills degrade; knowledge waits. Resume the maths next week and lose nothing but time.

Never optimise before publishing the baseline

Measure first, write the numbers down publicly, then optimise. Not just good engineering — it converts the project from “I built an order book” into “I made it 40× faster and here is the evidence,” which is a different sentence entirely.

Take the stepping stone, whatever it pays

If a fintech, bank, broker or exchange role appears at any point after week 36, take it and rebuild this plan around less time and vastly better information. Being inside the industry beats being outside it with better notes.

Question four is the important one

At every monthly checkpoint, ask what should be dropped. A plan with no mechanism for cutting scope is one that gets abandoned wholesale instead of trimmed.

Deliberately excluded, and it stays that way

Stochastic calculus beyond literacy. Machine learning and alpha research. Certifications. A master's degree, for now. Breadth of any kind. At five hours a week of genuine capacity, going wide means arriving at week 104 with nothing anybody will pay for.

Before week one

08 · Setup
  • Sit the Week 0 diagnostic closed-book and timed. Then a second pass with notes. Record both scores. The gap between them sets the length of the maths block, and inflating either number by twenty percent costs three months of planning error.
  • Pick your fixed evening hour and put it in the calendar as a recurring event. The consistency of the slot matters more than which hour it is.
  • Install the toolchain in week 1, not week 3: clang or gcc, CMake, GoogleTest, ASan and UBSan wired into the build from the first commit rather than retrofitted later.
  • Create the public repository now and make the first log entry before there is anything to report. Starting the habit on an empty week is much easier than starting it on a good one.
  • Order Harris. It is the only book you need in the first six months and shipping to Jakarta is slow.
  • Name the thing you are giving up, out loud. Ten hours a week has to come from somewhere. If nothing is being cut, the hours are not real and this page is fiction.

Built for a floor of 10 hours a week from a physics degree gone cold and five years of JVM engineering. Both assumptions are worth checking against four weeks of real logged data before committing to the 104-week length.

The C++ pivot is the expensive branch and the one place the recommendation was overridden. Week 34 exists so that decision gets revisited once, loudly, with a defined alternative — rather than quietly, by attrition.
