Week 0 Diagnostic

Twenty points across calculus, linear algebra, probability, differential equations and numerics. It exists for one reason: to set the length of the mathematics block before the plan commits to a length it cannot keep.

Time90 min
ConditionsClosed book
Total20 pts
Probability8 pts

Rules

Protocol

Ninety minutes, closed book. No notes, no internet, no calculator beyond arithmetic. Write the working, not just the answer — partial credit is real. Where you are stuck, write down what you would look up; that is diagnostic information too.

Pass 1 is closed-book and timed. It measures retention. Pass 2 comes afterwards, with notes, redoing everything missed. It measures re-learning speed. The gap between the two scores is the real signal.

Do not cheat. The only person you can mislead here is your own schedule. A score inflated by twenty percent buys a three-month planning error that surfaces around week 30, which is the worst possible time to discover it.

Section A — Calculus

4 points
A1Evaluate ∫₀∞ x·e−x dx.
A2Differentiate ln(cos x). Then expand ex·cos x as a Taylor series up to and including the x³ term.
A3State the value of ∫₋∞∞ e−ax² dx for a > 0, and sketch how it is derived.
A4For f(x,y) = x²y + y³: find the gradient, then the directional derivative at (1,2) in the direction of (3,4).

Section B — Linear algebra

3 points
B1Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of [[2,1],[1,2]].
B2Define a positive semi-definite matrix. Give one practical test for it. Explain why a covariance matrix is always PSD.
B3What does the singular value decomposition give you, geometrically? Name one use for it in finance.

Section C — Probability

8 points · weighted

Weighted heaviest on purpose. This is the working mathematics of market microstructure, and it is the section physics graduates are typically weakest at — statistical mechanics is not probability theory.

C1Two fair dice are rolled. Given that the sum is 8, what is the probability that at least one die shows a 3?
C2X ~ Exponential(λ). Compute E[X] and Var[X] from the definition. Then show P(X > s+t | X > s) = P(X > t), name the property, and say in one sentence why it matters.
C3X, Y are iid Uniform(0,1). Compute E[max(X,Y)] and P(X + Y < 1).
C4Define conditional expectation E[X | Y]. State the tower property. Then use it: a coin with unknown bias p ~ Uniform(0,1) is flipped once — what is the probability of heads?
C5Sₙ is a simple symmetric random walk. Give E[Sₙ] and Var[Sₙ], and compute P(S₁₀ = 0). Define a martingale and say whether Sₙ is one.
C6State the Central Limit Theorem precisely. Then approximate P(sum > 20) for 100 iid variables with mean 0 and variance 1.
C7A stock's mid-price is 100.00 with a spread of 0.02. You buy at the ask and immediately sell at the bid — what is the loss? Now suppose you can instead post at the bid and get filled half the time, crossing the spread the other half: what is the expected cost per round trip? Deliberately not a textbook question — it is the kind of reasoning the job is made of, and scoring zero here is expected.
C8What is the difference between correlation and independence? Give a concrete example of two variables that are uncorrelated but not independent.

Section D — Differential equations

2 points
D1Solve dy/dx = −ky with y(0) = y₀. Then solve y'' + 3y' + 2y = 0.
D2Write down the one-dimensional heat equation. In one sentence, say what it describes physically — and, if you know, what it has to do with option pricing.

Section E — Numerical & computational

3 points
E1Describe how to estimate π by Monte Carlo. How does the error scale with the number of samples N, and what is the practical consequence of that scaling?
E2Why does 0.1 + 0.2 != 0.3 in floating point? What is catastrophic cancellation, and give an example where it would destroy a financial calculation.
E3Compute a running mean and variance over a stream of prices in one pass, without storing the data. Describe an approach. What goes wrong with the naive E[X²] − E[X]² formula?

Scoring

Calibration

Score each part generously for correct method and harshly for hand-waving. Record three numbers in the log: pass 1 total, pass 1 Section C, and pass 2 total.

Use whichever row is worse. Probability dominates because probability is the job.

Reading the gap between passes

  • Gap under 15% — it genuinely is not there. Take the longer block. No shame in it: the degree bought re-learning speed, not retained knowledge.
  • Gap over 30% — it is all still there, just cold. Take the shorter block and lean on problem sets rather than lectures.

If Section C is much weaker than the rest

Expected, and fine. Spend the whole block on Blitzstein & Hwang and ignore everything else. Rusty calculus costs almost nothing in this field. Weak probability costs everything.
